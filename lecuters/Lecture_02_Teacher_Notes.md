## PART 1 — WHAT IS A METRIC? (10 minutes)

### Definition:
> A metric is a quantifiable measure used to track and assess the status of a specific process or outcome.

### The difference between data and a metric:

| Data | Metric |
|------|--------|
| 50 employees left this year | 10% attrition rate |
| 30 days to fill a vacancy | 30 days average time-to-hire |
| Training cost Rs. 5,00,000 | Rs. 1,000 training cost per employee |

**Teaching point:** A metric gives data **meaning** by putting it in relation to something — a total, a time period, a benchmark.

---

## PART 2 — CORE HR METRICS (20 minutes)

### 1. Attrition / Turnover Rate

```
Attrition Rate = (Number of employees who left / Average headcount) × 100
```

**Example:**
- 50 employees left
- Average headcount = 500
- Attrition Rate = (50/500) × 100 = **10%**

**Ask students:** Is 10% good or bad? (Revisit Lecture 1 lesson — context matters)

---

### 2. Time to Hire

```
Time to Hire = Date of offer acceptance − Date job was posted
```

**Why it matters:** Long time-to-hire = lost productivity, candidate drop-off, business disruption.

**Industry benchmark:** Typically 20–40 days depending on role level.

---

### 3. Cost per Hire

```
Cost per Hire = (Internal recruiting costs + External recruiting costs) / Total hires
```

**Components:**
- Job advertising
- Recruiter salaries
- Interview time
- Background checks
- Onboarding costs

---

### 4. Absenteeism Rate

```
Absenteeism Rate = (Days absent / Total working days) × 100
```

**Why it matters:** High absenteeism = low engagement, health issues, management problems.

---

### 5. Training Cost per Employee

```
Training Cost per Employee = Total training expenditure / Number of employees trained
```

---

### 6. Revenue per Employee

```
Revenue per Employee = Total revenue / Total number of employees
```

**Why it matters:** Measures workforce productivity at the organizational level.

---

### 7. Offer Acceptance Rate

```
Offer Acceptance Rate = (Offers accepted / Offers made) × 100
```

**Low rate signals:** Compensation issues, poor candidate experience, employer brand problems.

---

## PART 3 — THE CONTEXT PROBLEM (10 minutes)

### Write on the board:

```
METRIC ALONE          vs.          METRIC IN CONTEXT

Attrition = 10%                    Attrition = 10%
                                   Industry average = 15%
                                   → We are doing BETTER than average

Attrition = 10%                    Attrition = 10%
                                   Last year = 5%
                                   → We are getting WORSE
```

**Teaching point:** Every metric needs at least one of these:
1. **Benchmark** — how does it compare to industry/competitors?
2. **Trend** — how has it changed over time?
3. **Target** — how does it compare to our goal?

---

## PART 4 — METRICS vs. ANALYTICS (10 minutes)

This is a critical distinction for MBA students.

| Metrics | Analytics |
|---------|-----------|
| Describe what happened | Explain why it happened |
| Backward looking | Forward looking |
| Single number | Pattern across multiple variables |
| Example: 10% attrition | Example: Attrition is highest among employees aged 25–30 with <2 years tenure in the Sales department |

**Ask students:**
> *"Your HR dashboard shows attrition jumped from 8% to 15% this quarter. Is that metrics or analytics?"*

Answer: That is **metrics** (descriptive). Analytics would be finding out **why** it jumped.

## COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS TO ADDRESS

1. **"High attrition is always bad"** — Not necessarily. Some attrition is healthy (removing poor performers). The question is: *who* is leaving?

2. **"More metrics = better HR"** — Wrong. Too many metrics create noise. Focus on metrics that connect to business outcomes.

3. **"HR metrics are only for HR"** — Wrong. Revenue per employee, cost per hire, and attrition directly affect business performance and should be reported to the CEO.

## STUDENT NOTES VERSION (Short/Exam-friendly)

**HR Metric** = a quantifiable measure used to track HR processes and outcomes.

**Key HR Metrics:**
- Attrition Rate = (Leavers / Avg. headcount) × 100
- Time to Hire = Offer date − Job posting date
- Cost per Hire = Total recruiting costs / Total hires
- Absenteeism Rate = (Days absent / Total working days) × 100

**Every metric needs context:** benchmark, trend, or target.

**Metrics vs. Analytics:** Metrics tell you WHAT. Analytics tells you WHY and WHAT NEXT.
