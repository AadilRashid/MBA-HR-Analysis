## PART 1 — START WITH HR (5–10 minutes)

### What is HR?
Human Resources = the people working within an organization **and** the systems/practices used to manage them.

### Key HR Activities:
- Recruitment
- Selection
- Training & Development
- Compensation & Benefits
- Performance Management
- Promotion
- Retention
- Workforce Planning

### How to open the class (DO NOT lecture — ask):
> *"What decisions does an HR manager make on a daily basis?"*

Let students generate the list on the board. Then say:

> *"Every one of these decisions can potentially involve data."*

This sets up the entire course in one sentence.

---

## PART 2 — WHAT IS ANALYTICS? (10 minutes)

### Your personal definition:
> Analytics = systematic examination of data to discover useful patterns, relationships and insights that can support decisions.

### The key distinction to draw on the board:

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Data** | Raw facts and observations |
| **Information** | Data organized to answer a basic question |
| **Insight** | Something meaningful discovered through analysis |
| **Decision** | Action taken based on the insight |

### Write this on the board:
```
DATA → INFORMATION → INSIGHT → DECISION
```

This is your conceptual framework for the entire course. Return to it every lecture.

---

## PART 3 — HR ANALYTICS DEFINED (10 minutes)

### Teaching definition:
> HR Analytics is the use of data and analytical methods to understand workforce-related issues and support better decisions about people and organizational performance.

### What it is NOT:
- It is not simply HR + Excel
- It is not necessarily HR + AI

> *"The technology is secondary. The important question is: what decision are we trying to improve?"*

This is consistent with how Wharton frames people analytics — using data to improve how people are managed.

---

## PART 4 — THE OPENING EXAMPLE (15 minutes)

### Write on the board:
```
COMPANY X
1,000 employees
100 employees left
```

Ask: *"What can we say?"*

Students will say: **10% attrition.**

Then ask: *"Is 10% good or bad?"*

They will probably say bad. Then ask:

> *"Compared with what?"*

**Teaching point:** 10% might be:
- High in one industry
- Normal in another
- Low in another

> **A number without context is often not an insight.**

This is your first analytics lesson. It takes 5 minutes and students remember it for the whole semester.

---

## PART 5 — DATA → METRIC → ANALYTICS (10 minutes)

### Write this progression on the board:

```
DATA
100 employees left.
        ↓
METRIC
10% attrition rate.
        ↓
ANALYSIS
Which employees are leaving? When? From which departments?
        ↓
INSIGHT
Employees with <2 years tenure have disproportionately high attrition.
        ↓
DECISION
Improve early-career retention programs.
```

> This is the entire philosophy of the course in one example.

---

## PART 6 — THE FOUR TYPES OF ANALYTICS (10 minutes)

### Do NOT make them memorize the names first. Ask the questions:

| Question | Type |
|----------|------|
| **What happened?** | Descriptive Analytics |
| **Why did it happen?** | Diagnostic Analytics |
| **What is likely to happen?** | Predictive Analytics |
| **What should we do?** | Prescriptive Analytics |

### Common misconception to address:
Most HR departments today are still at **descriptive** level (headcount reports, turnover %). The goal of this course is to move toward **diagnostic and predictive**.

## OPENING ACTIVITY (10 minutes)

Give students this scenario:

> *"You are the HR Manager of a company with 500 employees. Your CEO walks in and says: 'Our best people are leaving. Fix it.' What is the first thing you do?"*

Let them discuss in pairs for 3 minutes, then share.

**Purpose:** Show that before any analysis, you need to define the problem. This is the first step in any analytics project.

## STUDENT NOTES VERSION (Short/Exam-friendly)
