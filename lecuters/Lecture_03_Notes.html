Formal Definitions

Descriptive Analytics

The process of using statistical techniques to summarize, organize, and present historical workforce data in a meaningful way. Descriptive analytics answers the question What happened? and forms the foundation upon which diagnostic and predictive analytics are built. It includes measures of central tendency, frequency distributions, and visual representations of data. (Davenport & Harris, 2007)

Measure of Central Tendency

A statistical value that represents the centre or typical value of a dataset. The three primary measures are: mean (arithmetic average), median (middle value when data is ordered), and mode (most frequently occurring value). In HR analytics, the choice between mean and median is important when data contains outliers such as executive salaries. (Field, 2018)

Frequency Distribution

A tabular or graphical representation showing how often each value or range of values occurs within a dataset. In HR analytics, frequency distributions are used to understand the composition of the workforce — for example, how many employees fall into each department, job level, or age group.

Workforce Profile

A structured summary of the demographic and organizational characteristics of an organization's employees at a given point in time. Includes headcount by department, gender distribution, age distribution, job level breakdown, and tenure profile. Used as the starting point for any HR analytics project. (CIPD, 2020)

Pivot Table

An interactive data summarization tool in spreadsheet software that allows users to rapidly aggregate, group, and cross-tabulate large datasets without writing formulas. In HR analytics, pivot tables are used to calculate headcount, average salaries, attrition rates, and satisfaction scores across multiple dimensions simultaneously.

Attrition Breakdown

The disaggregation of an overall attrition rate into sub-groups — by department, job level, gender, tenure band, or other variables — to identify where employee departures are concentrated. Attrition breakdown is the first step in moving from descriptive to diagnostic analytics, as it reveals patterns that the overall rate conceals. (Fitz-enz & Mattox, 2014)

Variance / Standard Deviation

Statistical measures of how spread out values are around the mean. Standard deviation is the square root of variance and is expressed in the same units as the original data. In HR analytics, a high standard deviation in salary data indicates large pay disparity within a group, which may signal equity issues or inconsistent compensation practices.

Cross-tabulation

A method of jointly analysing two or more categorical variables by displaying their frequency distribution in a matrix format. In HR analytics, cross-tabulation is used to examine relationships such as attrition by department AND job level simultaneously, revealing intersectional patterns not visible in single-variable analysis.

What is Descriptive Analytics?

Descriptive analytics is the first and most common level of HR analytics. It summarizes what the workforce looks like and what has happened.

DESCRIPTIVE ANALYTICS ANSWERS:
  → How many employees do we have?
  → What is our attrition rate?
  → What does our workforce look like?
  → How are salaries distributed?
  → Which department has the most turnover?

It does NOT answer:
  → Why is attrition high?     (Diagnostic)
  → Who will leave next?       (Predictive)
  → What should we do?         (Prescriptive)
Most HR dashboards and reports are descriptive analytics. It is necessary but not sufficient for good decision-making.

Key Statistical Measures

MeasureFormula / ExcelUse in HR
Mean=AVERAGE(range)Average salary, avg tenure
Median=MEDIAN(range)Typical salary (avoids outlier distortion)
Mode=MODE(range)Most common performance rating
Min / Max=MIN() / =MAX()Salary range, tenure range
Count=COUNT(range)Headcount
CountIf=COUNTIF(range,"value")Headcount by dept / gender
AverageIf=AVERAGEIF(range,"value",avg_range)Avg salary by dept
Std Dev=STDEV(range)Salary dispersion

Mean vs. Median — Why It Matters

Example: 5 employee salaries (INR)
  30,000 / 32,000 / 35,000 / 38,000 / 1,50,000

  Mean   = (30+32+35+38+150) / 5 = 57,000
  Median = 35,000

The mean is pulled up by the one high salary.
The median better represents the typical employee.
Always use median when reporting salary data. Use mean only when the distribution is roughly symmetric with no extreme outliers.

Using Pivot Tables in HR Analytics

Pivot tables let you summarize 500 rows of data in seconds.

To create a Pivot Table in Excel:
  1. Click anywhere in the dataset
  2. Insert → PivotTable → New Sheet
  3. Drag fields to Rows / Columns / Values

Common HR Pivot Table setups:
  Rows: Department
  Values: COUNT of Employee_ID
  → Gives headcount by department

  Rows: Department
  Values: AVERAGE of Salary
  → Gives avg salary by department

  Rows: Department, Columns: Attrition
  Values: COUNT of Employee_ID
  → Gives attrition breakdown by department

  IT            Lowest

  Finance       Mid-range
  Operations    Mid-range

This pattern raises a question:
  WHY is Sales attrition so much higher?

That question moves us from DESCRIPTIVE
to DIAGNOSTIC analytics — Lecture 4.
The value of descriptive analytics is not the numbers themselves — it is the questions those numbers raise.
