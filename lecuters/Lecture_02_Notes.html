Formal Definitions

HR Metric

A quantifiable, standardized measure used to track, evaluate, and benchmark the effectiveness of human resource processes and outcomes. HR metrics translate workforce activity into numerical indicators that can be monitored over time and compared against internal targets or external benchmarks. (Boudreau & Ramstad, 2007)

Attrition / Employee Turnover

The rate at which employees leave an organization over a given period, expressed as a percentage of the average workforce. Voluntary attrition refers to employee-initiated departures; involuntary attrition refers to organization-initiated separations. High attrition imposes direct costs (recruitment, onboarding) and indirect costs (knowledge loss, team disruption). (Armstrong & Taylor, 2020)

Time to Hire

The elapsed time between the posting of a job vacancy and the acceptance of an offer by a candidate. It is a measure of recruitment efficiency and directly affects organizational productivity, as unfilled roles represent lost output. Distinct from time to fill, which measures from requisition approval to offer acceptance.

Cost per Hire

The total expenditure incurred by an organization to recruit and onboard one new employee, including internal costs (recruiter time, HR administration) and external costs (advertising, agency fees, background verification). It is a key indicator of recruitment efficiency and budget utilization. (SHRM, 2016)

Absenteeism

The habitual or frequent absence of an employee from work beyond what is considered normal or acceptable, excluding authorized leave. Absenteeism rate is calculated as a proportion of scheduled working days lost and serves as a proxy indicator of employee engagement, health, and organizational climate. (Cascio, 2018)

Revenue per Employee

A productivity metric calculated by dividing total organizational revenue by the total number of full-time equivalent employees. It provides a high-level measure of workforce efficiency and is used to benchmark organizational performance against industry peers. (Fitz-enz, 2010)

Workforce Planning

The systematic process of analysing current workforce composition, forecasting future talent requirements, and developing strategies to close identified gaps between supply and demand. It aligns human capital strategy with organizational objectives over short, medium, and long-term horizons. (CIPD, 2020)

Offer Acceptance Rate

The proportion of employment offers extended by an organization that are accepted by candidates, expressed as a percentage. A declining offer acceptance rate signals issues with compensation competitiveness, employer brand, candidate experience, or the quality of the recruitment process. (SHRM, 2016)

Part 2 — Core HR Metrics (20 min)

1. Attrition / Turnover Rate

Attrition Rate =
  (Employees who left / Avg headcount) × 100

Example:
  50 left, avg headcount = 500
  = (50/500) × 100 = 10%
ASK: "Is 10% good or bad?" → Revisit Lecture 1: context matters.

2. Time to Hire

Time to Hire =
  Date of offer acceptance − Date job posted

Benchmark: typically 20–40 days
Why it matters: long time = lost productivity,
candidate drop-off, business disruption.

3. Cost per Hire

Cost per Hire =
  (Internal + External recruiting costs)
  / Total hires

Components: job ads, recruiter salaries,
interview time, background checks, onboarding.

4. Absenteeism Rate

Absenteeism Rate =
  (Days absent / Total working days) × 100

High rate signals: low engagement,
health issues, management problems.

5. Training Cost per Employee

Training Cost per Employee =
  Total training expenditure
  / Employees trained

6. Revenue per Employee

Revenue per Employee =
  Total revenue / Total employees

Measures workforce productivity at org level.

7. Offer Acceptance Rate

Offer Acceptance Rate =
  (Offers accepted / Offers made) × 100

Low rate signals: compensation issues,
poor candidate experience, employer brand.

Part 3 — The Context Problem (10 min)

METRIC ALONE          vs.    METRIC IN CONTEXT

Attrition = 10%              Attrition = 10%
                             Industry avg = 15%
                             → BETTER than average

Attrition = 10%              Attrition = 10%
                             Last year = 5%
                             → Getting WORSE

Every metric needs at least one of:

1. Benchmark — vs. industry / competitors
2. Trend     — vs. last period
3. Target    — vs. our goal
A metric without context is just a number. This is the same lesson from Lecture 1 — reinforce it here.

Part 4 — Metrics vs. Analytics (10 min)

MetricsAnalytics
Describe what happenedExplain why it happened
Backward lookingForward looking
Single numberPattern across variables
10% attritionAttrition highest among 25–30 yr olds with <2 yrs tenure in Sales
ASK: "Your HR dashboard shows attrition jumped from 8% to 15% this quarter. Is that metrics or analytics?"
Answer: Metrics (descriptive). Analytics = finding out WHY it jumped.

In-Class Exercise (15 min)

Using the KashmirTech dataset:

1. How many employees are in each department?
2. What is the overall attrition rate?
3. Which department has the highest attrition?
4. What is the average salary?
5. What additional information would you need to explain the attrition difference between departments?
Question 5 is the most important. It teaches students to think beyond the number — the core analytics mindset.

What to Write on the Board

HR METRICS

METRIC = Data + Context + Meaning

KEY METRICS:
- Attrition Rate
- Time to Hire
- Cost per Hire
- Absenteeism Rate
- Training Cost per Employee
- Revenue per Employee

METRIC NEEDS:
1. Benchmark (vs. industry)
2. Trend     (vs. last period)
3. Target    (vs. our goal)

METRICS ≠ ANALYTICS
Metrics  = WHAT
Analytics = WHY + WHAT NEXT

Common Misconceptions to Address

✗ "High attrition is always bad"
  → Some attrition is healthy (removing poor
    performers). The question is: WHO is leaving?

✗ "More metrics = better HR"
  → Too many metrics create noise. Focus on
    metrics that connect to business outcomes.

✗ "HR metrics are only for HR"
  → Revenue per employee, cost per hire, and
    attrition directly affect business performance
    and should be reported to the CEO.

Questions to Ask MBA Students

  • "Which of these metrics does your organization currently track?"
  • "If you could only track 3 HR metrics, which would you choose and why?"
  • "How would you explain attrition rate to a CEO with no HR background?"
