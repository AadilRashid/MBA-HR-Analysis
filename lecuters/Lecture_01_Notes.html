Formal Definitions

Human Resources (HR)

The organizational function responsible for managing the employee lifecycle — from recruitment and selection through development, performance management, compensation, and separation — with the objective of maximizing employee contribution to organizational goals. (Armstrong & Taylor, 2020)

Analytics

The systematic computational analysis of data or statistics, used to discover, interpret, and communicate meaningful patterns in data, and to apply those patterns towards effective decision-making. (Davenport & Harris, 2007)

HR Analytics / People Analytics

The application of statistical methods, data mining, and predictive modelling to human capital data in order to improve workforce-related decisions, enhance organizational performance, and generate competitive advantage through people. (Fitz-enz & Mattox, 2014)

Descriptive Analytics

The use of historical data to summarize past events and present a clear picture of what has occurred within an organization. Answers: What happened? Examples: headcount reports, turnover dashboards, absenteeism summaries.

Diagnostic Analytics

The examination of data to understand the causes and contributing factors behind observed outcomes. Answers: Why did it happen? Involves drill-down analysis, correlation, and root-cause investigation.

Predictive Analytics

The use of statistical algorithms, machine learning, and historical data to forecast future outcomes and identify the probability of future events. Answers: What is likely to happen? Example: predicting which employees are at risk of leaving.

Prescriptive Analytics

The use of optimization and simulation techniques to recommend specific actions that will achieve desired outcomes. Answers: What should we do? Example: recommending targeted retention interventions for high-risk employees.

Human Capital

The collective knowledge, skills, abilities, experience, and attributes embodied in individuals that are relevant to economic activity and organizational performance. Treated as an asset that can be invested in, measured, and managed. (Becker, 1964; Pease et al., 2012)

What is HR?

Human Resources = people working within an organization + the systems and practices used to manage them.

Key HR Activities:

  • Recruitment & Selection
  • Training & Development
  • Compensation & Benefits
  • Performance Management
  • Promotion & Retention
  • Workforce Planning

Core Framework

DATA        → Raw facts and observations
INFORMATION → Data organized to answer a basic question
INSIGHT     → Something meaningful discovered through analysis
DECISION    → Action taken based on the insight
This framework applies to every HR analytics problem. Return to it throughout the course.

What is HR Analytics?

HR Analytics is the use of data and analytical methods to understand workforce-related issues and support better decisions about people and organizational performance.

HR Analytics = DATA + ANALYSIS
                    ↓
             BETTER PEOPLE DECISIONS
                    ↓
          BETTER ORGANIZATIONAL OUTCOMES

The Opening Example

Company X — 1,000 employees — 100 left

Attrition Rate = 10%

Is 10% good or bad?
→ Depends on industry benchmark
→ Depends on historical trend
→ Depends on WHO is leaving

A number without context is not an insight.

Data → Decision Progression

DATA
100 employees left.
        ↓
METRIC
10% attrition rate.
        ↓
ANALYSIS
Which employees are leaving? When? Which dept?
        ↓
INSIGHT
Employees with <2 years tenure have
disproportionately high attrition.
        ↓
DECISION
Improve early-career retention programs.

Four Types of Analytics

QuestionType
What happened?Descriptive Analytics
Why did it happen?Diagnostic Analytics
What is likely to happen?Predictive Analytics
What should we do?Prescriptive Analytics
Most HR departments today operate at the descriptive level. The goal of this course is to move toward diagnostic and predictive.
