Formal Definitions

Human Resources (HR)

The organizational function responsible for managing the employee lifecycle — from recruitment and selection through development, performance management, compensation, and separation — with the objective of maximizing employee contribution to organizational goals. (Armstrong & Taylor, 2020)

Analytics

The systematic computational analysis of data or statistics, used to discover, interpret, and communicate meaningful patterns in data, and to apply those patterns towards effective decision-making. (Davenport & Harris, 2007)

HR Analytics / People Analytics

The application of statistical methods, data mining, and predictive modelling to human capital data in order to improve workforce-related decisions, enhance organizational performance, and generate competitive advantage through people. (Fitz-enz & Mattox, 2014)

Descriptive Analytics

The use of historical data to summarize past events and present a clear picture of what has occurred within an organization. Answers: What happened? Examples: headcount reports, turnover dashboards, absenteeism summaries.

Diagnostic Analytics

The examination of data to understand the causes and contributing factors behind observed outcomes. Answers: Why did it happen? Involves drill-down analysis, correlation, and root-cause investigation.

Predictive Analytics

The use of statistical algorithms, machine learning, and historical data to forecast future outcomes and identify the probability of future events. Answers: What is likely to happen? Example: predicting which employees are at risk of leaving.

Prescriptive Analytics

The use of optimization and simulation techniques to recommend specific actions that will achieve desired outcomes. Answers: What should we do? Example: recommending targeted retention interventions for high-risk employees.

Human Capital

The collective knowledge, skills, abilities, experience, and attributes embodied in individuals that are relevant to economic activity and organizational performance. Treated as an asset that can be invested in, measured, and managed. (Becker, 1964; Pease et al., 2012)

Teaching philosophy: make students better managers and critical consumers of evidence — not memorizers of definitions. (Wharton model)

Part 1 — What is HR? (5–10 min)

Definition: Human Resources = people working within an organization + the systems/practices used to manage them.

Key HR Activities:

  • Recruitment & Selection
  • Training & Development
  • Compensation & Benefits
  • Performance Management
  • Promotion & Retention
  • Workforce Planning
ASK: "What decisions does an HR manager make on a daily basis?"
→ Let students generate the list on the board.
THEN SAY: "Every one of these decisions can potentially involve data."
Do NOT lecture for 20 minutes on HR definitions. One question sets up the entire course.

Part 2 — What is Analytics? (10 min)

Definition: Systematic examination of data to discover useful patterns, relationships and insights that support decisions.

DATA        → Raw facts and observations
INFORMATION → Data organized to answer a basic question
INSIGHT     → Something meaningful discovered through analysis
DECISION    → Action taken based on the insight
Write this on the board: DATA → INFORMATION → INSIGHT → DECISION. Return to this framework every lecture.

Part 3 — HR Analytics Defined (10 min)

Teaching definition: HR Analytics is the use of data and analytical methods to understand workforce-related issues and support better decisions about people and organizational performance.

It is NOT:   HR + Excel
It is NOT:   HR + AI (technology is secondary)

The key question is always:
"What decision are we trying to improve?"
Consistent with Wharton framing — using data to improve how people are managed.

Part 4 — The Opening Example (15 min)

COMPANY X
1,000 employees
100 employees left
ASK: "What can we say?" → Students: 10% attrition.
ASK: "Is 10% good or bad?" → They say bad.
ASK: "Compared with what?"

Teaching point: 10% might be high in one industry, normal in another, low in another.

A number without context is often not an insight. This is your first analytics lesson — students remember it all semester.

Part 5 — Data → Metric → Analytics (10 min)

DATA
100 employees left.
        ↓
METRIC
10% attrition rate.
        ↓
ANALYSIS
Which employees are leaving? When? Which dept?
        ↓
INSIGHT
Employees with <2 years tenure have
disproportionately high attrition.
        ↓
DECISION
Improve early-career retention programs.
This is the entire philosophy of the course in one example. Write it on the board and leave it there.

Part 6 — Four Types of Analytics (10 min)

Do NOT make them memorize names first. Ask the questions:

QuestionType
What happened?Descriptive Analytics
Why did it happen?Diagnostic Analytics
What is likely to happen?Predictive Analytics
What should we do?Prescriptive Analytics
Most HR departments today are still at descriptive level (headcount reports, turnover %). Goal of this course: move toward diagnostic and predictive.

Opening Activity (10 min)

Scenario: "You are the HR Manager of a company with 500 employees. Your CEO walks in and says: 'Our best people are leaving. Fix it.' What is the first thing you do?"

Pairs discuss 3 min → share with class.
Purpose: show that before any analysis, you must define the problem. This is Step 1 of every analytics project.

Questions to Ask MBA Students

  • "Can you think of an HR decision in your organization made without data? What was the outcome?"
  • "What is the difference between an HR report and HR analytics?"
  • "Why do you think most companies are still at the descriptive level?"
